# Resume Checklist

A one-page guide you can use without an agent. Based on Wulfie Bain's CV thread.

## What a CV is for

A CV cannot capture who you are as a person. It is a reductionist document, and that is fine. Its job is to show that you have relevant experience and can communicate it well, so the reviewer decides it is worth having a live chat. Make it easy for them to see what you would bring to the team and why you should talk.

## Before you write

Get a warm referral if you can. If you don't know anyone, apply online anyway. That is how the author got into OpenAI.

## The four checks

### 1. Keep it concise

- [ ] It is ideally one page. A second page is acceptable if relevance demands it, not because a template has room.
- [ ] Every line helps this application. Cut repeated responsibilities, old jobs with no signal, and filler summaries.
- [ ] The document shows you can prioritise and write clearly.

### 2. Cut irrelevant experience

- [ ] Every item makes its relevance to this role clear. Don't make the reviewer connect the dots.
- [ ] Anything not adding signal is gone. High school achievements are usually not relevant once you have real experience, even if you are proud of them.
- [ ] The skills section adds something the bullets don't already say.

### 3. Show impact, not action

- [ ] Bullets say why the work mattered, not only what you did.
- [ ] Impact is quantified where it can be: time saved, latency reduced, revenue gained, users reached, cost cut, quality improved.
- [ ] The metric is real. Do not invent one; find it or leave it out.
- [ ] The kind of impact fits the role. Sales talks revenue. Engineering talks speed, reliability, or scale. Research talks findings and what changed.

| Weak | Better |
| --- | --- |
| Re-architected an agent. | Re-architected the agent, cutting latency by 2s and raising customer satisfaction. |
| Managed a team. | Led a team of 6, shipping the migration two weeks early. |
| Improved the onboarding process. | Cut onboarding time from 10 days to 3, lifting new-hire retention. |

### 4. Make it easy to skim

- [ ] The strongest evidence comes first under each experience.
- [ ] The few things that must survive a five-second scan are bolded.
- [ ] Bullets are short enough to take in at a glance.
- [ ] Titles and dates are easy to find.

## The ten-second test

Hand the CV to someone for ten seconds, then take it away. Ask them:

1. What role does this person appear to want?
2. What is the strongest evidence that they can do it?

If they hesitate, the CV is not making its argument yet.

## The point of all four

Clear communication, relevance, and evidence of impact matter throughout the application, not only in the CV. The CV's only job is to get you the conversation where you can show the rest.
