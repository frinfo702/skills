# ML research evidence

What the rest of the skill cites as `E<n>`: studies of AI research agents and advice from ML researchers on how empirical ML research goes well or badly. Collected 2026-10-31; arXiv IDs were checked to exist. Use the mechanisms, not the exact numbers.

## Ideas versus execution

- **E1. Ideas that look better on paper can do worse when executed.** In a blind study with 100+ NLP researchers, LLM ideas were rated more novel than experts' ideas (p<0.05) and slightly less feasible ([Si, Yang, Hashimoto 2024, 2409.04109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04109)). When 43 researchers then executed ideas for 100+ hours each, LLM ideas' scores dropped more than human ideas' and the ranking flipped on many metrics ([The Ideation-Execution Gap, 2506.20803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20803)).
- **E2. LLM ideas are narrow in kind.** They lean toward "bridging" and "integrating methods" and cover a narrower distribution of opportunity patterns and paradigms than human ideas ([2607.01233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01233)). Under explicit compute, API and time budgets, all tested harnesses scored low on scenario novelty ([AlphaIdeaBench, 2610.33644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33644)).

## Execution is the bottleneck

- **E3. Implementation and reproduction limit automated research.** The best agent reproduced 21% of ICML 2024 papers from scratch ([PaperBench, 2504.01848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01848)); coding agents produced fabricated or invalid experimental results in 80% of cases on open-ended ML research tasks ([MLR-Bench, 2505.19955](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19955)); a survey of 28 AI-scientist papers attributes failures to weak implementation and verification ([2506.01372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01372)). An independent evaluation of The AI Scientist found 42% of experiments failed on code errors ([2502.14297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14297)).
- **E4. Starting from a baseline paper works better than starting from nothing.** Jr. AI Scientist begins from a given baseline paper and its code, analyses its limitations, then iterates experiments toward an improvement ([2511.04583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04583)). Karpathy's recipe and Jia-Bin Huang's advice both put the data, a dumb baseline and an end-to-end skeleton first ([Karpathy 2019](https://karpathy.github.io/2019/04/25/recipe/), [Huang](https://github.com/jbhuang0604/awesome-tips/blob/main/steady-progress.md)).
- **E5. Search pays off when fitness is cheap and checkable.** AlphaEvolve's evolutionary search works with an automatic evaluator ([2506.13131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13131)); pure evolution plateaus early without research and debugging ([DeepEvolve, 2510.06056](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06056)). AI agents beat human experts at 2-hour budgets but gain less from more time; at 32 hours humans did twice as well ([RE-Bench, 2411.15114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15114)). Cheap proxies make more iterations possible, but only if they rank like the real setting.

## Guarding the loop

- **E6. Self-evaluation is not verification.** Automated reviews, Elo self-ratings and self-reported scores were not independent truth; outside re-runs and wet-lab tests decided ([2502.14297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14297), [AI co-scientist 2502.18864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18864)).
- **E7. Immutable experiment cards stop drift.** Writing predictions and results to a fixed-schema card that cannot be rewritten kept an autonomous research loop honest; about three quarters of its hypotheses were falsified and recorded as such ([2608.07542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07542)).
- **E8. Attribute failures before reacting.** Splitting a failed experiment into competing explanations (hypothesis, experiment design, implementation) instead of one reflection raised the share of runs producing metric-bearing output from 42% to 92% ([SAGE, 2606.31478](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31478)).
- **E9. Success can be forecast in part.** A fine-tuned 8B model predicted which of two ideas would succeed empirically with 77.1% accuracy, above GPT-5's 61.1% ([2605.21491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21491)). Useful as a prioritization signal, not a verdict.

## Transfer and pattern matching

- **E10. Conventional base plus one unusual combination.** Across 17.9 M papers, the highest-impact work combined highly conventional knowledge with an intrusion of atypical combinations, about twice as likely to be a hit ([Uzzi et al. 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24159044/)). Disruptive work pays off later and less reliably ([Wu, Wang, Evans 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30760923/)).
- **E11. Analogies work when matched on structure.** Representing work by purpose and mechanism finds better analogies than surface retrieval, and helped people produce more creative ideas ([Hope et al. 2017, 1706.05585](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.05585)). Gating cross-domain analogies by typed problem–method–metric–claim structure filtered candidates about 17:1 while over 83% of accepted ideas had a quantitative falsifier ([2608.20361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20361)); retrieving solution mechanisms from similar problems improved grounded ideation ([IdeaScientist, 2610.04074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04074)).
- **E12. A transplanted mechanism carries its assumptions.** ViT worked with large-scale pretraining ([2010.11929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)); a transfer must check that the source's preconditions (data scale, label quality, resolution, compute) hold in the target.

## Advice from ML researchers

- **E13. Goal-driven beats idea-driven; problem choice is the biggest lever.** Work toward a goal others are not pursuing rather than the idea everyone is about to have; keep a research notebook; a common failure is switching problems too often, not too rarely ([Schulman](http://joschu.net/blog/opinionated-guide-ml-research.html)). Idea selection multiplies impact ([Jason Wei](https://www.jasonwei.net/blog/practicing-ai-research)).
- **E14. Keep a list of important problems and a way to attack them** ([Hamming](https://www.cs.virginia.edu/~robins/YouAndYourResearch.html)); study why promising ideas failed to build taste ([Nielsen](https://michaelnielsen.org/blog/principles-of-effective-research/)).
- **E15. Practical methodology.** Set the metric and target first, build an end-to-end system early, instrument it to find which component limits performance, then change one thing at a time ([Goodfellow, Bengio, Courville, *Deep Learning*, ch. 11](https://www.deeplearningbook.org/contents/guidelines.html); [Google Deep Learning Tuning Playbook](https://github.com/google-research/tuning_playbook)).
- **E16. Narrow closed loops with human feedback work.** Agent Laboratory's quality improved significantly with human feedback at each stage ([2501.04227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.04227)); FutureHouse's Robin ran a short hypothesis–experiment–analysis loop with humans doing the experiments ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10652-y)).
